"App 'NoveltyWealth' is live on iOS and App Store. Offers two main parts: account aggregation with insights, and advice/transactions based on those insights. Features bank analysis, goal-based planning, and an AI chatbot for queries."

unit_economics_and_capital_efficiency:
"Proposing two subscription tiers: a 'light' version for insights (less than 200 INR/month) and a second advisory layer with execution (500-600 INR/month for the entire family)."

  [founder]: Medium to long-term will be organic and referral-based, building trust. For initial acquisition, we're using inorganic digital marketing and seeing 3-4x better top-of-funnel conversions than market norms. Once we have 200-500 paying customers, we'll pursue B2B2C partnerships with corporates as a financial wellness benefit for employees. We'll ask corporates to introduce our product to their employees, not bear costs. Parallelly, we're building organic content like newsletters and articles to build trust.

  [founder]: The initial analysis version will be a free trial for 7-15 days. We want users to explore and see the unique value before incorporating payments, which will start September 1st.

  [founder]: Yes, that's why we need guidance on our GTM roadmap and help in recruiting the right people, so founders can focus on fundraising and exploring new channels.

  [founder]: After 1000, we'll introduce new features, start with plan implementation, and the advisory layer, with monetization beginning September 1st.
  [founder]: We anticipate 60-70% of revenue from advisory subscriptions and 30-40% from existing customers introduced to other products. We need to find channels with high volume for fast initial app adoption and free trials.

market_structure_and_timing:
  [mentor]: How do you define your ideal customer persona? Is it a young starter or someone in their mid-thirties with debt?
  [founder]: Our ideal customer has a career for 4-5 years, accumulated 15-20 lakh+ in savings, is earning and saving serious money, and is time-crunched due to growing career and family responsibilities. They need an expert eye on their finances.
  [mentor]: So your user is basically proactive, conscious about wealth and investments, and actively seeking a solution?
  [founder]: Not fully conscious, but they care about their money even if they can't put effort into it. We've built in short-term, medium-term, and long-term engagement hooks. For example, a bank analysis layer categorizes transactions so users can track spending on things like travel or food, which serves as a high-interest 'hooky' element, alongside long-term wealth management solutions.
  [mentor]: There's a genuine need for this service in India, especially for your target segment, given the current poor financial health, lack of emergency funds/insurance, and growing debt culture.
  [mentor]: Retail user acquisition cost in Fintech is very high, and engagement/retention are challenging for all Fintech apps. Keeping users opening your app weekly or bi-weekly is difficult. Many players have tried retirement planning, but user acquisition and retention costs remain high.
  [mentor]: Subscription businesses are difficult in India; people readily pay for entertainment but show skepticism for financial services. This is why most FinTechs pivot to lending models, though this might not align with your current direction. The corporate B2B2C angle, while good, has been explored for over a decade and is challenging to scale effectively due to low interest from HR departments.
  [founder]: Money (a competitor) started with advisory but couldn't scale the human advisory layer, so they pivoted to a distributor and broker business. Their tracking layer is now just for customer acquisition, not their core business.
  [mentor]: Micro-saving is a good distribution tactic, but the high transaction costs for small volumes make it more of a tax strategy than a sustainable business model.
  [founder]: SEBI regulations improved last year, now allowing SEBI-registered providers to offer non-SEBI products with transparency and disclosure, making it a truly one-place personal finance platform (e.g., credit cards, home loans, insurance). SEBI has also given a green flag to AI tools for advisory, holding the entity responsible for the AI's advice. We are training our AI with guardrail checks (two-agent AI model) to ensure accuracy and compliance.

MVP:
  [mentor]: Your UI currently looks like a hardcore FinTech app and is too technical for basic users. It needs to be 'stupid simple,' intuitive, and engaging, similar to a friend explaining things, with proactive AI using micro-animations instead of text. While features are good, the UI needs significant changes to make it fun and easy to use for new investors. Features like retirement planning need more interactive back-and-forth UI.
  [founder]: We have a goal section in the app.
  [founder]: Our transaction link is directly with CAMS and KFintech, allowing users to buy and sell any mutual fund bought on any platform, unlike platforms like Groww or Zerodha that restrict this.
  [founder]: Our app provides a comprehensive view of everything in finances: investments, bank accounts, taxation, financial goals, and overall net worth. It drills deep into each element, showing asset distribution, risk profile, and goal-based asset allocation. It also analyzes cash flow, income/expense splits, and allows AI chat queries for specific spending details ('Zomato vs. Swiggy') or 'what-if' investment scenarios. It tracks a 'net worth to expense ratio' for retirement readiness, adjusted for inflation. A 'wealth check-up' feature proactively identifies inefficiencies and suggests personalized optimizations, like moving excess cash from a bank to an arbitrage fund for better returns. It also analyzes portfolio quality (e.g., too many mutual funds, low-quality funds) and identifies the user's investor style (e.g., active trader) to provide relevant advice, like suggesting mutual funds if active trading isn't beating the index.
  [founder]: Our upcoming retirement planning feature will be a chat-based conversation, not just a calculator. It will leverage linked data, nudge users about their savings profile, and be more visual, interactive, and timeline-driven to show year-by-year asset allocation and planning, simplifying complex variables for the user.
  [founder]: While calculators are good for engagement, they often don't convert users effectively. Our service focuses on helping users achieve the right asset allocation and investment, which directly stems from this planning.
  [founder]: Our advisory will primarily be AI-based. AI will replace 60-70% of advisors' time (sales and reporting), leaving human advisors for the initial core 5-10% advisory work. Over time, AI will replace even that minimal human input, handling confirmations, providing rationale, and facilitating transactions. SEBI regulations now permit AI tools for advisory, with the entity responsible for the AI's advice; we use a two-agent AI model for guardrail checks.

  [founder]: Our business model projects 60-70% of revenue from advisory subscriptions and 30-40% from commissions on other financial products (e.g., credit cards, home loans, insurance), which SEBI regulations now allow us to offer. We aim for an average revenue of 1,500-2,000 INR per customer per year. We've activated credit card partnerships, leveraging banking analysis to match users with suitable cards that offer significant savings (e.g., 10% on Swiggy), and are building an advisory layer for this.
